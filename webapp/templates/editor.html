Step 1 - {{ source_lang_name }} source: fix the ASR transcript against what's actually said. Timing here is real (straight from faster-whisper), so click-to-seek is accurate. Low-opacity words are ones it was less confident about - once you save a correction for a segment, its words go back to full opacity - you've read them, the flag's done its job. If a whole segment came out garbled or missed real speech, you don't have to retype it by hand - alt+t re-runs ASR on just that segment's own audio and drops the fresh result in, real timing included.

Step 2 - {{ target_lang_name }} translation: correct the text that actually gets spoken and subtitled. If you fixed the source in step 1, hit "Re-translate from corrected source" (alt+r) first to get a fresh translation of it before hand-editing - a translation that's fallen behind a later source correction gets a dashed blue outline automatically, so you don't have to remember to check. Word timing here is estimated (proportioned across the segment by character count) since there's no real per-word alignment for translated text - the dashed underline marks that.

The empty dashed boxes in either step are real gaps (a full second or more of non-speech, detected from actual ASR timing) - type a tag like [MUSIC] or [PAUSE] into one if it needs a caption note.

Corrections save themselves - a segment saves automatically a couple of seconds after you stop typing in it, so there's nothing to click. ctrl+s forces it immediately, and ctrl+shift+s also re-renders that segment's audio and rebuilds the mixed track. The topbar always shows whether everything's saved.

The transcript reads as one continuous document, not a stack of separate chunks - a flagged segment gets a dotted underline instead of a callout box; hover it (or press alt+↑/↓ to jump between flagged spots) to see why. Use ctrl+enter on a word to start a new paragraph where it makes sense.

When every flagged segment looks right, use "Approve & mark ready for delivery" above to release it to the client.

Full editor guide & style guide → - flag glossary, timing/overlap explainer, caption style rules, all in one page, opens in a new tab.

The Spell check button (step 2 only) flags possibly-misspelled words in red - click a flagged cell for suggestions, click one to replace it, "Ignore" to dismiss, or just type over it yourself. It's spelling only, not grammar - there's no free grammar checker wired up, so read it yourself too.

Playback never auto-restarts. Once a clip finishes, the Ereri mark covers the player - click it (or press play) to start again. It never covers the video before you've played it or while it's playing, and never appears in anything delivered to a client.

Plain space / arrows / [ / ] only act as playback shortcuts when you're not actively typing in a cell - click outside or press Esc first. Shift+space is the opposite: it only fires WHILE a cell is focused. Everything below "Cell editing" also only fires while a cell IS focused, and none of it touches your browser's own copy, paste, select-all or undo - those still work normally inside a cell.

Why some of these aren't on the keys you might expect

A handful of keys - Ctrl+F, Ctrl+T, Ctrl+D, Ctrl+H, Ctrl+J, Ctrl+L and similar - are reserved by the browser itself (find, new tab, bookmark, history, downloads, address bar) and a web page can't override them. Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+A and Ctrl+Z are left alone on purpose so normal copy, paste, select-all and undo keep working inside a cell. Everything above does the same job using an Alt-based key instead.
