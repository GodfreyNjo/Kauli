1. What Kauli actually does

Kauli transcribes, translates, and optionally dubs audio/video between Swahili, English and Kikuyu. The real pitch to a client: AI drafts it in minutes, a human edits every segment before it ships - never AI-only, never fully manual either. Kikuyu specifically uses a human transcriber, not a speech-recognition model, because no production-ready one exists yet for that language.

Three real service levels a client picks from: transcription (text only), translation (transcript + translated text/subtitles, no spoken audio), and dub (translation plus a synthesized or human-recorded voice track). Add-ons on top: human voice-over (a real actor instead of AI TTS), video deliverables (burned captions or a fully muxed dubbed video), rush turnaround.

2. The three account roles

Client
Places orders, pays, downloads deliverables, messages staff on an order thread. Never sees Ereri or any other staff tooling.
Staff
Works the queue in Ereri, handles billing/CRM/exceptions. Two tiers: plain staff can do all real order work; admin staff additionally manage the Voice Talent roster and the admin panel (inviting new staff, promoting accounts). Being staff doesn't automatically mean admin - most contractors doing pure editing work don't need admin access.
Voice actor
A real third role, separate from staff - self-service login at /actor, sees only the orders they're personally assigned, can listen to the script and upload their finished recording. Never sees Ereri, billing, or any other order's data.

3. An order's real lifecycle, start to finish

  1. pending_payment - client has configured the order but hasn't paid yet.
  2. queued → processing - the AI pipeline is running (transcription, translation, and TTS if it's a dub order). This happens automatically once payment clears; no staff action needed here.
  3. awaiting_review - the AI draft is done and sitting in the staff queue. This is where Ereri work happens - every segment gets read and corrected by a human before it can ship.
  4. ready_for_delivery - a staff member hit "Finish job" in Ereri after every segment was reviewed. The client can now see and download their real deliverables.
  5. editor_returned / returned_to_client - a job got sent back instead of finished: editor_returned means an editor flagged it back to Ops (wrong language, no real audio, instrumental-only, sound-effects-only, or something else worth a second look - see the return reasons in the job-return form); returned_to_client means it went all the way back to the client because of a real problem with what they submitted.
  6. dead_letter - the automated pipeline itself failed and gave up after retrying. This needs a staff member to look at the real error and either retry it or investigate what actually broke (see /staff/exceptions).

A human always has the final say at every one of these steps - nothing ships to a client without someone actually looking at it first, regardless of how clean the AI draft came out.

5. Voice acting work

If you're a contracted voice actor: you log in at the same place staff and clients do, and land on /actor - your own dashboard, showing only orders assigned to you. You'll see the real script with timestamps, and an upload form for your finished recording. There's no automatic timeline-fitting - getting your take synced to the source video is real editorial work you do with your own tools before uploading; this is only where the finished result gets delivered from. Once uploaded, it becomes what the client actually downloads, replacing whatever AI-rendered dub existed before.

6. Billing & payments

Clients pay per audio-minute at published rates, plus any add-ons. Payment methods today: Paystack (card, M-Pesa, or Airtel Money, all live and real) and a direct M-Pesa STK-push option that's built but not yet live (shows as "coming" at checkout - don't tell a client it's available). Bank transfer exists as a manual, non-automated option.

Some clients run on a prepaid wallet instead of paying per order - their balance shows on their account, and a low-balance email goes out automatically once it drops under the threshold. Receipts are auto-generated and emailed on every successful payment; if email delivery isn't configured or fails, a receipt still exists and shows under "Receipts awaiting delivery" on /staff/billing for someone to forward by hand.

7. Common situations & how to actually handle them

Client says our email landed in spam
Ask them to mark it "not spam" and check they're looking at the right inbox - our real sending address is {{ contact_email }}, on our own authenticated domain, not a personal Gmail. If it's a NEW kind of email bouncing or consistently misfiring, that's worth flagging to Godfrey rather than guessing.
A YouTube-sourced order's embedded player shows "This content is blocked"
This is YouTube itself refusing the embed, not a bug in your correction work - Ereri has a real "Open on YouTube ↗" fallback link right under the player for exactly this. Word-level timing stays accurate either way, since it comes from the real audio, not the embedded player.
An order is stuck in "processing" for an unusually long time
Check /staff/exceptions - if it's genuinely failed, it'll show as dead_letter with a real error message, and there's a retry button. A long-running dub with voice cloning can legitimately take several minutes on this hardware - that alone isn't necessarily a problem.
A segment's translation looks wrong after correcting the Swahili
Hit Re-translate (Alt+R) - a source correction doesn't automatically retranslate if the English side was already hand-edited, specifically so nobody's manual work gets silently overwritten. A dashed blue outline on English text means it's now stale relative to a newer source correction.
A client wants a refund or says the order didn't meet what was promised
There's no automatic refund system - this is a real, case-by-case human decision (redo, credit, or refund). Don't promise a specific outcome before checking with Godfrey on anything beyond a routine redo.

8. Where everything technically lives

Only relevant if you're doing technical/ops work, not day-to-day editing.

  • Code: a private GitHub repo. Real changes get committed and pushed there, then pulled onto the server.
  • The app itself: runs in a Docker container on a Hetzner Cloud server. Nothing runs on anyone's personal laptop any more.
  • The public site: reached through a Cloudflare Tunnel, not a direct connection to the server - there's no open port an outside attacker could even find.
  • Backups: the database and every real client file sync to Cloudflare R2 storage automatically every night.
